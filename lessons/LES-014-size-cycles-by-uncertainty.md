# Lesson: Size cycles by uncertainty, not by minimum size

## Observation / evidence

Interpreted evidence: `UND-PROCESS-002` (from `SRC-PROCESS-002`).

SimplyStore dogfooding showed a useful early tendency toward very small cycles while the system and durability model were unfamiliar. As understanding stabilized, the same tendency risked turning into unnecessary ceremony: related work that could be evaluated coherently remained split simply because small cycles had become the default rhythm.

## Lesson

Small cycles are a response to uncertainty, not a universal virtue. Split when one result can invalidate another, when risk/irreversibility warrants a separate boundary, or when the human needs a distinct evaluation decision. Once assumptions and invariants are stable, prefer a larger coherent cycle over repeated planning/review overhead.

A small evidence-producing probe inside a cycle does not imply that the outer cycle itself must be small.

## Scope

Normal Spiral cycle planning and re-planning.

## Confidence / limits

Moderate-to-high confidence from dogfooding. Very large, cross-repository, and operational cycles may still need stronger size constraints for reasons not yet observed.

## Proposed consequence

Add an explicit cycle-sizing rationale to planning and clarify in normative guidance that the correct boundary follows uncertainty and evaluation coherence.
