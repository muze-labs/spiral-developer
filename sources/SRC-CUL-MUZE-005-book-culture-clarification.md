# Source: Human clarification of Muze culture after Programming for Wizards review

## Source kind

Human clarification in the Spiral Developer project discussion after candidate cultural principles were distilled from *Programming for Wizards*.

## What was actually expressed or observed

The reviewer confirmed the proposed deeper synthesis of Muze engineering culture and clarified the scope/trade-offs of several candidate principles:

- **Innovation happens elsewhere** is an observation Muze tries to keep in mind across its work, while accepting that client constraints can limit openness or external participation.
- **User/data ownership** is a strong Muze preference. It can influence which clients or projects Muze is willing to accept, but Muze cannot always control ownership architecture in client work.
- **Keep ideas high in the stack** is intentionally used as a productive way to avoid architecture astronautics.
- Muze is generally **skeptical of frameworks** because their fit is often generic rather than specific to the problem. Frameworks also have real advantages, especially easier handover between developers with different engineering cultures, and those advantages can outweigh the disadvantages in a particular project.
- Muze prefers to **avoid NIH when possible without damaging what is being built**.
- **Getting something working at the right time is more important than perfection. Timing is part of the deliverable.** Efficient AI may change this historical trade-off because “perfect” no longer necessarily competes with “now” in the same way.
- Many of these ideas reflect Muze's understanding **before efficient use of AI was possible**. They should therefore be treated as historically situated and revisable while the industry learns under changed production economics.
- **Progressive enhancement** is a useful approach wherever it can work, not only on the Web.
- The reviewer confirmed the underlying synthesis: problems are shaped by representations and assumptions; make those visible, move boundaries rather than pile on machinery, put choices where they can still change, expect to be wrong, and preserve the ability to replace the answer.
