# Source: Human direction on replaceable warning profiles

## Source kind

Connected human design discussion.

## What was actually expressed or observed

The warning heuristics should be their own replaceable and alterable thing, separate from both Spiral Developer core and engineering culture.

The agreed operational concerns are:

- premature closure: notice when an ambiguous need is being collapsed into the first plausible interpretation;
- authority: capability or inference does not itself grant permission to act;
- evidence: a confident explanation must not outrun the evidence supporting it;
- composition: individually acceptable automated actions may compose into system-level behavior nobody actually verified;
- optionality: notice when a fallback, override, export path, alternative workflow, or other escape route is removed, without treating preservation as mandatory;
- meaningful exclusion: surface materially consequential cases where a feature may exclude or disadvantage a meaningful class of users, while avoiding noise for trivial edge cases;
- model closure: notice when an implementation forces human reality into a narrower model than the requirement actually requires, such as assuming every person can provide both a first name and a family name.

The signals should prompt inspection rather than automatically prohibit an action. Significance matters: professional ethical concerns should surface when consequences are meaningful, not on every small change.

The philosophical observation that choosing not to act can itself have consequences should remain a matter for human professional education and judgment rather than becoming an automated Spiral warning.

## Integrity / version

Captured prospectively from the human direction that caused this process change.

## Provenance confidence

Explicit.

## Limitations / uncertainty

The source establishes the desired separation and the initial warning set. It does not establish that these warning definitions are complete or permanently correct; warning profiles are intended to evolve independently.
