# Understanding: Guided intake establishes the project frame before brownfield cycles

## Interpretation

The requested intake is not a one-time architecture inventory and not a new maturity framework. Its purpose is to establish a human-confirmed project frame of reference before ordinary Spiral cycles begin in an existing project.

That frame combines three kinds of knowledge:

1. **Desired direction** — purpose, goals, important outcomes/metrics, consequential commitments, constraints, and future direction that the human/project considers meaningful.
2. **Investigation lenses** — explicitly selected reusable risk-discovery and metric profiles that help the AI know what kinds of evidence or gaps are worth looking for, plus explicit exclusions and project-specific additions.
3. **Reality comparison** — an AI investigation of the current project against the confirmed frame, producing candidate risks, metric gaps, missing measurements, and uncertainties for human disposition.

The AI may suggest common answers and suitable profiles to reduce setup cost, but it does not define project priorities or silently convert profile contents into requirements. The human confirms the intake frame and later decides which evidenced candidate risks are important now, accepted, deferred, rejected, or missing.

The intake should also teach Spiral progressively: briefly explain why each question matters at the point of use instead of requiring the human to study the methodology first.

## Deliberate limits

For the first version:

- keep answers and profiles as ordinary Markdown;
- reuse project context rather than introduce a new causal artifact class;
- do not create a universal maturity score or project-health score;
- do not require every project to use the same metrics or risk lenses;
- keep "other", "not sure", and "not relevant" as first-class answers;
- treat recurring custom answers as possible future process-learning evidence, not automatic global configuration changes.

## Expected effect

A new human collaborator should be able to start Spiral responsibly through a short guided conversation. A new AI collaborator should then inherit durable project-level context from the repository and have a more justified basis for choosing where to investigate before proposing the next cycle.
